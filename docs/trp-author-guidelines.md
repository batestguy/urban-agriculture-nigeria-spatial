# Town and Regional Planning — author guidelines and policies (verbatim)

**Journal:** Town and Regional Planning
**Publisher:** University of the Free State, Bloemfontein, South Africa
**ISSN:** 1012-280X (print) · 2415-0495 (online)
**Frequency:** biannual; the second issue is often a guest-edited special issue
**Editorial contact:** Department of Town and Regional Planning, University of the Free State,
PO Box 339, Bloemfontein 9300, South Africa · +27 (0)51 401 3546
**Endorsement:** South African Council for Planners (SACPLAN), the statutory body regulating the
planning profession in South Africa

---

## Focus and scope

> Town and Regional Planning is a South African accredited journal dedicated to independently
> reviewed research on town, urban, and regional planning. The journal publishes peer-reviewed
> Review Articles, Research Articles, Perspective Articles, and Book Reviews. Manuscripts should
> be written in a clear, accessible style, suitable for both specialists and non-specialists. The
> format for these categories can be found in the Guidelines to Authors. The journal is endorsed
> by the South African Council for Planners (SACPLAN) the statutory body that regulates the
> planning profession in South Africa.

## Fees and open access

> There are no submission fees or article-processing charges.
>
> Town and Regional Planning is a Diamond Open Access journal, meaning that all published content
> is freely available to readers without subscription fees or access charges, and authors are not
> required to pay article processing charges.

## Copyright and licensing (from `/about/submissions`)

> **Copyright:** Copyright is transferred to the author(s) when an article is accepted for
> publication.
> **Publishing rights:** When an author/s publish an article in *Town and Regional Planning*, the
> author/s enter into a non-exclusive publishing agreement. This means that author/s may upload a
> second copy to institutional repositories.

> Articles published in Town and Regional Planning are archived on the journal's website via the

## Author guidelines (from `/about/submissions`)

> Town and Regional Planning publishes articles in English, with a desired length of **4,000 to
> 8,000 words (excluding references)**. Articles must be written in **third person**.
>
> Submit a typed copy in electronic format via the online portal.
>
> **Formatting:** MS Word, **Times New Roman, font size 12, single spacing**. Paragraphs should
> start on a new line, not indented. All illustrations, figures, and tables should be placed
> within the text. Tables must be in editable format, not images, with text in **Times New Roman,
> font size 10, single spacing**. Table borders must be ½ pt width.
>
> Articles on relevant topics in town, urban, and regional planning, well-presented, clearly
> written, and proofread, will be considered for publication.
>
> The Editor may alter articles to align with the journal's style. If referees suggest large
> changes, the article will be returned to the author for revisions.
>
> Copyright is transferred to the author(s) upon article acceptance.
>
> Titles must be concise and informative, with suitable headings and sub-headings.
>
> The **abstract (250 words)** should start with 2–3 sentences introducing the field and research
> problem, followed by 2–3 sentences outlining research methods, a one-sentence main finding(s)
> (or conclusions, in the case of a Review Article), and 2–3 sentences placing the findings in
> context. Abstracts do not include references.
>
> Provide relevant keywords in English after the abstract.
>
> The **introduction** should overview the topic, present prior research, and then justify the
> research's relevance explaining why it is important to be addressed right now. Link it to
> current issues or gaps in existing literature and/or theories.
>
> **Literature reviews** should identify international research on similar topics and indicate a
> clear gap in research articles related to international cutting-edge research. Authors should
> clearly demonstrate how their research relates to that of other scholars on similar topics.
>
> The **study area must be after the literature review and must not be part of the methodology
> section.**
>
> The **methods section** should describe what was done to answer the research question, describe
> how it was done, justify the experimental design, and explain how the results were analysed. It
> must include the following sections: 1) Research design, 2) Population, sampling, and response
> rate, 3) Data collection, 4) Data analysis and how to interpret the results, 5) Limitations to
> the study (can be in discussion or conclusion).
>
> The **discussion section** should explain and evaluate what you found, showing how the results
> relates to your literature review and paper topic, and making an argument in support of your
> overall conclusion.
>
> The **conclusions** must not be a summary of the results but should focus on the contribution of
> the findings to the field, highlighting their relevance both within the discipline and across
> other areas. Begin with broad implications, followed by specific contributions. The significance
> should be communicated in a way that engages not only specialists but also a broader audience,
> using clear, accessible language.
>
> For sections and subsections use **Arabic numbers with full stops in-between, i.e. 1. Followed
> by 1.1 and 1.1.1 up to the maximum of three levels.** After that use an (a).
>
> Source references in the text must be in the **Harvard style** of referencing. i.e. `(Healey,
> 1996: 201-202)`.
>
> **In-text page numbers:** Include page numbers to in-text references when incorporating a direct
> quotation into a sentence, paraphrase a passage, summarise an idea from a particular page,
> mention statistics, or you wish to direct the readers to a specific page. Page numbers are not
> necessary if you are referring to the entire work as a whole. Use a colon to indicate the page
> number e.g.: `(Chunga et al., 2016: 5-7)`.
>
> **Use of et al.:** 3 authors, or more — first citation is immediately truncated to the first
> author's surname and the abbreviation "et al." follows.
>
> Foot- and endnotes are likewise done in the Harvard style of referencing and be included at the
> bottom of the page.
>
> The **references list** (in the journal's inhouse Harvard style of referencing) must contain all
> the relevant information, and be listed alphabetically according to the names of the authors,
> i.e. `HEALEY, P. 2026. Collaborative planning. London: McGraw-Hill.`
>
> URLs for the references of internet documents cited in the text and listed in the references
> must accompany the article. Also, indicate the date that the internet sites were visited.
>
> Abbreviations must be limited and only used for corporations etc. in general use, then only
> after it was written out in full at first, with the abbreviation in brackets. After this the
> abbreviated form is used.
>
> Words in other languages and stereotyped Latin terms such as *per se* must be in italics.
> Italics must be used sparsely. Emphasis must be in single inverted commas.
>
> Diagrams, maps, and photos should be submitted as **TIF or JPEG (300 dpi)** and captions must be
> provided in the text. Computer graphics are welcome but must be camera-ready on A4 paper with
> clear text and markings.
>
> Tables and Figures containing statistics, research output and conceptual frameworks must be in
> editable format in Word or Excel and not as image files.
>
> Indicate the origin of the article, i.e. if it was presented at a congress, or from a master's
> or PhD study. Articles must be unpublished and not under consideration elsewhere.
>
> Authors may suggest three potential referees (outside their institution).
>
> All submissions must be accompanied by a signed Author Agreement and Publishing Agreement.

### AI and Generative-AI Use (summary paragraph in the guidelines)

> Town and Regional Planning journal permits the limited use of generative AI tools (e.g.
> ChatGPT) for language editing and grammar support only. AI tools may not generate substantive
> content, analysis, or data. Any use of AI must be transparently declared in the manuscript (see
> Declaration of AI Use in the Authors Agreement). Authors remain fully responsible for the
> accuracy, originality, and integrity of their work.

### Note on the abstract, on length, and on the call for non-specialists

The journal twice asks for prose "intelligible to specialists and non-specialists alike" — in the
scope statement and in the conclusion guidance. It also asks for a **250-word unstructured**
abstract, not a structured one.

## Submission preparation checklist (from `/about/submissions`)

> All submissions must meet the following requirements.
>
> - The text is single-spaced; uses a 12-point font; Times New Roman; employs italics, rather than
>   underlining; and all illustrations, figures, and tables are placed within the text at the
>   appropriate points, rather than at the end.
> - The text adheres to the stylistic and bibliographic requirements outlined in the Author
>   Guidelines.
> - The submission file is in Microsoft Word document file format.
> - The ORCID's for individual authors have been verified and included in the article, as well as
>   in the online profile.
> - The correct sequence for items in each reference complies with the inhouse Harvard
>   referencing style required by the journal (see online archive for examples).
> - The references list contains all the relevant information, and is listed alphabetically
>   according to the names of the authors.
> - Only sources cited in the text appear in the reference list, and all references listed are
>   cited in the text.
> - The valid DOI number for each source in the reference list are provided.
> - Where available, URLs for the references have been provided.
> - Any use of AI has been transparently declared in the Declaration of AI Use section within the
>   Author Agreement. **It confirms that any use of generative AI tools (e.g. ChatGPT) has been
>   limited to language editing and grammar support only. AI has not been used to generate
>   substantive content, analysis, or data.** The Author(s) accept full responsibility for the
>   accuracy, originality, and integrity of the work.
> - The Comments to the Editor state the origin of the article (e.g., conference paper, part of a
>   master's or PhD study, independent work, or funded project). Formal evidence is attached to
>   verify the claim (e.g. official letter from the institution confirming enrolment or candidacy;
>   a valid institutional link to the completed project's repository or depository; a stamped
>   registration or completion certificate).
> - The Author Agreement and Publishing Agreement have been completed, signed by all authors, and
>   uploaded with the submission.

## Article categories (from `/about/submissions`)

Four categories are published; the relevant one for this manuscript is **Research articles**:

> **Research articles.** These are original research manuscripts. The work should report
> scientifically sound research and provide a substantial amount of new information. The article
> should include the most recent and relevant references in any applicable field of scholarship,
> i.e. town, urban and regional planning.
> *Format:* the format can be found in the Guidelines to Authors. Will be peer-reviewed.

The others are Review articles (systematic review or literature review), Perspectives (with a
100-word biography, four "Highlights", and a 5-figure/table cap), Commentaries (editorial review
only, no abstract) and Book reviews.

## AI and Generative-AI use — full policy (from `/AI`)

> **1. Purpose & Scope**
> This policy outlines the responsible use of generative Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools
> (e.g. ChatGPT, Bard, DALL·E, MidJourney) in the preparation, review, and editorial handling of
> manuscripts submitted to the Town and Regional Planning Journal. While AI tools can assist with
> language editing and routine tasks, the Journal requires that all intellectual, analytical, and
> interpretative contributions to planning scholarship remain the responsibility of human
> authors, reviewers, and editors.
>
> **2. Authors**
> *2.1 Acceptable Use*
> - Authors may use AI tools for language polishing, grammar correction, reference formatting, or
>   stylistic editing, particularly where English is not the author's first language.
> - AI may not be used to generate substantive content such as problem statements, literature
>   reviews, methods, analysis, findings, policy recommendations, or interpretations.
> - AI must not be used to create or manipulate maps, figures, diagrams, or data unless it forms
>   part of a clearly explained and justified research method.
>
> *2.2 Disclosure*
> - Any use of AI tools must be disclosed in a dedicated section entitled **Declaration of AI Use
>   before the references**.
> - Authors must specify: 1. The tool(s) used and version (if known); 2. The purpose of its use;
>   3. A statement that all outputs were critically reviewed, edited, and that the authors take
>   full responsibility for the final manuscript.
> - Example wording: "During the preparation of this work, the author(s) used [TOOL NAME] to
>   assist with [e.g. grammar and language editing]. After using this tool, the author(s) reviewed
>   and edited the content and take full responsibility for the work."
> - AI tools cannot be listed as authors or co-authors.
>
> *2.3 Restrictions*
> - AI-generated content must not be submitted without critical human oversight and editing.
> - Synthetic data generated by AI (e.g. simulated survey results) are not permitted unless
>   methodologically justified and transparently reported.
> - Authors remain accountable for plagiarism, factual accuracy, and proper citation.
>
> **3. Peer Reviewers** — manuscripts and their files must not be uploaded into AI tools; reviews
> must rest on human expertise; AI may not be used to evaluate research quality, validity or
> originality.
>
> **4. Editors** — editors may not upload manuscripts or reviewer reports into AI systems;
> editorial decisions must rest on human scholarly judgment.
>
> **5. Compliance & Updates**
> - Manuscripts may be screened for signs of AI misuse (e.g. fabricated references, inconsistent
>   writing patterns).
> - Non-compliance with this policy may result in rejection, correction, or retraction.

## Author Agreement (2026 form, `/libraryFiles/downloadPublic/115`)

Fields: title; then, for each of up to five authors, title/name/initials/surname, workplace and
capacity, postal address, mobile number, e-mail address and **ORCID**; then:

- **A: Conflict of interest** — tick "no conflict" or declare one.
- **B: Authors agreement** — corresponding author bears full responsibility; the research is not
  published or under consideration elsewhere; all listed authors contributed significantly and
  approve the submission; no author-list changes without the Editor's approval.
- **C: Declaration regarding AI and AI-assisted technologies** — the form asks for:
  > During preparation of this manuscript the author(s) used [NAME TOOL / SERVICE] ________ to
  > [REASON] ________. The following paragraphs and/or sections are relevant: ________. After
  > using this tool/service, the author(s) reviewed and edited the content as needed and take(s)
  > full responsibility for the content of the publication. It is estimated that ____ % of this
  > manuscript was informed using AI and AI-assisted technologies.

  Signature block per author (place, date, signature).
